										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<img width="1024" height="743" src="https://fadeawayworld.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Nick-Young-1024x743.jpg" class="attachment-large size-large wp-post-image" alt="Washington Wizards shooting guard Nick Young during game against the Phoenix Suns at the US Airways Center. The Suns defeated the Wizards 104-88." style="display:block; max-width:100%; height:auto; margin:0 0 1em 0;" decoding="async" /><p>Former NBA champion <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/nick-young" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Nick Young</a> has accused the league of purposely hiding footage of a spectacular dunk he threw down on <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/lebron-james" target="_blank" rel="noopener">LeBron James</a> during his rookie season. The former <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/nba/washington-wizards" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Washington Wizards</a> star thinks the highlight deserved a lot more recognition, and he suggested the NBA was protecting the image of its biggest superstar.</p>
<p><a href="https://x.com/ClubShayShay/status/2110154867872563276?s=20" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">During a recent appearance on the Club Shay Shay podcast</a>, Young spoke about dunking on LeBron in the 2007-08 season when the four-time MVP was playing for the <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/nba/cleveland-cavaliers" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Cleveland Cavaliers</a>.</p>
<p><em>&#8220;I dunked on LeBron my rookie year, and they did the tape too. That&#8217;s when they was hiding the tapes. He tried to do the same thing, but I was younger then. So I was playing defense on him. He looked me up like, &#8216;What&#8217;s this rookie doing on me?&#8217; Shot the three, it was a long rebound, got it. Coast to coast. Tried to do that chase-down.&#8221;</em></p>
<p><em>&#8220;I was only, I was what, top 10? I thought I was going to be number one because of LeBron. It was number 10. I should&#8217;ve got number one. It was a random night. It was like a Wednesday. It&#8217;s LeBron getting dunked on, but it was around when they was hiding LeBron tapes.&#8221;</em></p>
<p><em>&#8220;He got dunked on by Jordan Crawford, and they hid the tape, and I was the second one, so it was like, he can&#8217;t keep getting the bears. It&#8217;s the face. It&#8217;s our future, so.&#8221;</em></p>
<p>The former Wizards star also connected his experience to another controversial incident involving LeBron and former NBA guard Jordan Crawford. Young was referring to the infamous 2009 incident at the LeBron James Skills Academy, where Crawford, who was a college basketball player, <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/nba/jordan-crawford-nike-owe-500-dunking-lebron-james" target="_blank" rel="noopener">reportedly dunked on LeBron during a pickup game</a>.</p>
<p>The incident became a big controversy because Nike representatives confiscated recordings of the play from people who had filmed it. Footage eventually surfaced online, which generated a lot of attention and fueled speculation that LeBron&#8217;s representatives were attempting to protect his reputation.</p>
<p>But there is an important distinction between Crawford&#8217;s incident and Young&#8217;s allegation.</p>
<p>While the confiscation of footage of the 2009 basketball play has been widely reported, Young hasn&#8217;t provided any evidence that the NBA suppressed his dunk. In fact, his own recollection suggests that the highlight was publicly featured, even if only at number 10, rather than the top position, which he thought it deserved.</p>
<p>Nevertheless, Young&#8217;s frustration is quite understandable from his point of view.</p>
<p>LeBron was basketball&#8217;s biggest star in the 2007-08 season, having led the Cavaliers to the 2007 NBA Finals. His signature chase-down block had become an important part of his reputation as an extraordinary athlete and defender.</p>
<p>For a rookie Young, successfully finishing a dunk with James chasing him would have been a significant achievement.</p>
<p>Nick Young eventually had a successful 12-year NBA career, playing for several teams, including the Wizards, <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/nba/los-angeles-lakers" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Los Angeles Lakers</a>, and <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/nba/golden-state-warriors" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Golden State Warriors</a>. He won an NBA championship with the Warriors in 2018 as he became a popular figure for his wild personality and scoring ability.</p>
<p>LeBron, meanwhile, has developed into one of the greatest players in NBA history, winning four NBA championships, four NBA MVP awards, and four Finals MVP awards, and establishing numerous all-time records.</p>
<p>Being dunked on occasionally is hardly unusual for a player who is constantly challenging shots at the rim, but Young thinks his memorable moment against LeBron deserved a much bigger spotlight.</p>
<p>Whether the NBA intentionally minimized the highlight is yet to be proven, but nearly two decades later, Nick Young still hasn&#8217;t forgotten what he calls an overlooked moment from his rookie season.</p>

Mandatory Credit: Kevin Jairaj-Imagn Images" style="display:block; max-width:100%; height:auto; margin:0 0 1em 0;" decoding="async" loading="lazy" /><p><a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/nba/dallas-mavericks" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Dallas Mavericks</a> forward <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/cooper-flagg" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Cooper Flagg</a> had a rookie season to remember, and he carried on from where he left off in preseason against the <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/nba/houston-rockets" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Houston Rockets</a> in Macau. Flagg certainly made a strong impression on former NBA star <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/demarcus-cousins" target="_blank" rel="noopener">DeMarcus Cousins</a>, who couldn&#8217;t stop singing his praises on <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YH1QiH2_6ZM&amp;t=4005s" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener"><em>Gil&#8217;s Arena</em></a>.</p>
<p><em>&#8220;He the real deal,&#8221; Cousins said. &#8220;&#8230; He damn near like a mixture of [Kevin Durant], [LeBron James], and Giannis [Antetokounmpo] all in one player.&#8221;</em></p>
<p>Cousins got some pushback for this from fellow former NBA player <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/iman-shumpert" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Iman Shumpert</a>, but he wasn&#8217;t backing down. The 36-year-old clarified he isn&#8217;t saying Flagg, who averaged 16.5 points, 5.0 rebounds, 5.0 assists, 1.5 steals, and 1.0 blocks per game against the Rockets, is as good as <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/kevin-durant" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Kevin Durant</a>, <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/lebron-james" target="_blank" rel="noopener">LeBron James</a>, or <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/giannis-antetokounmpo" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Giannis Antetokounmpo</a>, but he is a mixture of them.</p>
<p><em>&#8220;He&#8217;s also a 6&#8217;11&#8221;, 7-foot big getting downhill, Giannis,&#8221; Cousins said. &#8220;He&#8217;s also able to pass, shoot, dribble, playmake, Bron. He&#8217;s also the tallest wing. He&#8217;s a tall wing. He&#8217;s a tall wing scorer that&#8217;s able to put the ball on the floor and get to spots and shoot over his defenders, Kevin Durant. So, like I said, he&#8217;s a mixture of all three.&#8221;</em></p>
<p>Shumpert now understood the point Cousins was trying to make. He added, though, that Durant, James, and Antetokounmpo put together would look different.</p>
<p><em>&#8220;It&#8217;s just white,&#8221; Cousins said. &#8220;That&#8217;s why it&#8217;s hard to see it.&#8221;</em></p>
<p>Shumpert said Flagg being white isn&#8217;t the reason why. He appeared to be saying he&#8217;d have a similar reaction if three great white players were mentioned, but Cousins cut him off.</p>
<p><em>&#8220;It ain&#8217;t no white boys doing that,&#8221; Cousins said. &#8220;&#8230; Not like that. Not doing everything like that.&#8221;</em></p>
<p>Shumpert then brought up the likes of <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/larry-bird" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Larry Bird</a>, <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/toni-kukoc" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Toni Kukoc</a>, and <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/chris-mullin" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Chris Mullin</a>, and Cousins acknowledged they were great players. He made it clear, though, that they can&#8217;t do everything Flagg can. Bird is obviously the best of the bunch here, but Cousins feels he couldn&#8217;t get to the rim the way the 19-year-old can. Good luck trying to change his mind if you disagree.</p>
<p>Flagg&#8217;s ceiling is certainly very high. He averaged 21.0 points, 6.7 rebounds, 4.5 assists, 1.2 steals, and 0.9 blocks per game in 2025-26 after the Mavericks selected him with the No. 1 pick in the 2025 NBA Draft.</p>
<p>There was a lot of hype surrounding Flagg, and he lived up to it. He was named ROY after becoming the first rookie since <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/michael-jordan" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Michael Jordan</a> to lead his team in points, rebounds, assists, and steals.</p>
<p>ESPN&#8217;s <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/nba/dallas-mavericks/nba-insider-compares-cooper-flagg-to-lebron-james-says-hell-be-top-five-player-in-the-league-this-season" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Brian Windhorst also compared Flagg to LeBron James as a rookie</a>. Windhorst thinks the former Duke Blue Devil will become a top-five player in the NBA, but just isn&#8217;t sure how quickly that happens.</p>
<p>This 2026-27 NBA season does seem a bit early for that for Flagg. It&#8217;d be shocking if he&#8217;s in the same conversation as <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/nikola-jokic" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Nikola Jokic</a>, <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/shai-gilgeous-alexander" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Shai Gilgeous-Alexander</a>, <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/victor-wembanyama" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Victor Wembanyama</a>, <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/luka-doncic" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Luka Doncic</a>, and <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/anthony-edwards" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Anthony Edwards</a> as a sophomore.</p>
<p>Flagg should eventually be in that conversation, though. He is that good. The Mavericks weren&#8217;t supposed to get a generational talent this soon after stunningly trading Doncic to the <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/nba/los-angeles-lakers" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Los Angeles Lakers</a> in February 2025, but they have.</p>

					<description><![CDATA[<img width="1024" height="683" src="https://fadeawayworld.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Michael-Carter-Williams-in-action-for-the-Orlando-Magic-1024x683.jpg" class="attachment-large size-large wp-post-image" alt="Feb 1, 2020; Orlando, Florida, USA; Orlando Magic guard Michael Carter-Williams (right) drives around Miami Heat guard Goran Dragic (left) during the second half at Amway Center. 										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<img width="1024" height="683" src="https://fadeawayworld.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Michael-Carter-Williams-in-action-for-the-Orlando-Magic-1024x683.jpg" class="attachment-large size-large wp-post-image" alt="Feb 1, 2020; Orlando, Florida, USA; Orlando Magic guard Michael Carter-Williams (right) drives around Miami Heat guard Goran Dragic (left) during the second half at Amway Center. Mandatory Credit: Reinhold Matay-USA TODAY Sports" style="display:block; max-width:100%; height:auto; margin:0 0 1em 0;" decoding="async" loading="lazy" /><p>At the time he was drafted, Michael Carter-Williams was positioned to be a superstar in the NBA. As a bigger guard with phenomenal passing skills, the former ROTY was on track to be the face of the Philadelphia 76ers&#8217; franchise until injuries eventually derailed his career.</p>
<p>As a result of recurring foot and knee injuries, primarily to the left side of his body, Michael Carter-Williams found himself bouncing around several teams in the league. While this led to his eventual exit from the NBA in 2023, like many other role players, this experience allowed him to play alongside some exceptionally talented individuals.</p>
<p>This aspect of the journeyman&#8217;s career only came to light during his recent appearance on &#8220;<a href="https://www.instagram.com/p/Dd7mtc2mCy9/" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">Run It Back</a>,&#8221; where Michael Carter-Williams was asked to create a starting lineup (with a sixth man) comprising his former NBA teammates. Needless to say, the former first-round pick&#8217;s answer surprised the panel, as he shared:</p>
<p><em>&#8220;I think CP (Chris Paul) at the one. We&#8217;re talking prime, obviously? Yeah, CPs are in there. Dang. DWade, James Harden, Giannis, and Dwight.&#8221;</em></p>
<p>A lineup consisting of <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/chris-paul" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Chris Paul</a>, <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/dwyane-wade" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Dwyane Wade</a>, <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/james-harden" target="_blank" rel="noopener">James Harden</a>, <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/giannis-antetokounmpo" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Giannis Antetokounmpo</a>, and <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/dwight-howard" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Dwight Howard</a> is intimidating enough. But when asked to include his sixth man, Michael Carter-Williams confidently named <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/tag/jimmy-butler" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Jimmy Butler</a> to wrap up his rotation.</p>
<p>Having played on six different teams over his nine years in the NBA, Carter-Williams caught each player at different points in their careers. But when only considering their primes, this lineup is nothing short of monstrous.</p>
<p>At his peak, Chris Paul wasn&#8217;t just one of the best point guards in the NBA; he was <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/nba/la-clippers/blake-griffin-calls-chris-paul-a-top-5-point-guard-of-all-time" target="_blank" rel="noopener">one of the best of all time</a>. While possessing nifty ball-handling skills and a reliable shot from mid-range, Paul was known for his phenomenal court vision and unparalleled basketball IQ.</p>
<p>With averages of 19.8 points, 3.6 rebounds, 9.1 assists, and 2.5 steals (league-high) per game in the 2011-12 season, the year he finished third in MVP voting, Michael Carter-Williams couldn&#8217;t select a better player to be his point guard.</p>
<p>At shooting guard, the former ROTY selected Dwyane Wade. Although Wade was entering the final years of his career when he was teammates with Carter-Williams on the <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/nba/chicago-bulls" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Chicago Bulls</a>, during his prime, the two-time NBA champion was one of the league&#8217;s best shooting guards.</p>
<p>Like several players on this list, it is challenging to zero in on Wade&#8217;s &#8220;peak.&#8221; However, when noting his performance in the 2008-09 season, where he averaged 30.2 points (league-high), 5.0 rebounds, 7.5 assists, 2.2 steals, and 1.3 blocks per game and finished third in MVP voting, <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/nba-media/nba-legend-believes-dwyane-wade-better-than-lebron-james-primes" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Wade was unstoppable</a>.</p>
<p>Interestingly, Michael Carter-Williams selects James Harden at the 3, an odd pick considering that Harden is a combo guard. However, since a 3-guard lineup seems to be the former NBA player&#8217;s approach, the 2017-18 MVP deserves a mention.</p>
<p>Like Wade, simplifying Harden&#8217;s peak to just one season would be doing him a disservice. When gauging his dominance between 2017 and 2019, during which he averaged 33.7 points, 6.2 rebounds, and 7.9 assists per game while winning three scoring titles and one MVP award, Harden was in a league of his own.</p>
<p>As formidable as Michael Carter-Williams&#8217; backcourt is, his frontcourt is equally noteworthy. By including Giannis Antetokounmpo and Dwight Howard, the former NBA player essentially added two former DPOYs to fill out his big man rotation.</p>
<p>Giannis Antetokounmpo&#8217;s peak is difficult to gauge since he is only 31. However, when noting the 2019-20 season, a year before his title-winning campaign, where he was named MVP and DPOY after averaging 29.5 points, 13.6 rebounds, 5.6 assists, 1.0 steals, and 1.0 blocks per game, it is easy to see why Michael Carter-Williams selected him.</p>
<p>Much like Giannis, Dwight Howard&#8217;s best years also came before he won the NBA title. Although he won his championship with the <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/nba/los-angeles-lakers" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Lakers</a>, Howard was at the peak of his powers during his last four years with the Orlando Magic.</p>
<p>With averages of 20.6 points, 13.8 rebounds, 1.2 steals, and 2.6 blocks per game, Howard amassed several accolades, including three consecutive DPOY awards and two block titles. As a dominant force down low, Howard single-handedly led the <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/nba/orlando-magic" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Magic</a> to the NBA Finals in 2009. Even though he lost, his average of 15.3 rebounds in the playoffs is unreal.</p>
<p>As impressive as Michael Carter-Williams&#8217; starting lineup is, his first man off the bench, Jimmy Butler, also deserves some attention. Aside from being a five-time All-NBA selection, Butler has been one of the NBA&#8217;s most consistent playoff performers in the last decade.</p>
<p>Given his tendency to coast during the regular season, it is slightly difficult to narrow down his prime. However, during his stint with the <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/nba/miami-heat" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Miami Heat</a>, which included two runs to the NBA Finals, Butler&#8217;s averages of 24.7 points, 6.8 rebounds, 5.7 assists, 1.9 steals, and 0.6 blocks per game showcase why he deserves a place on this list.</p>

		<title>How Rob Dillingham Went From No. 8 Pick To Out Of The NBA</title>

					<description><![CDATA[<img width="1024" height="683" src="https://fadeawayworld.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Rob-Dillingham.--1024x683.jpg" class="attachment-large size-large wp-post-image" alt="Chicago Bulls guard Rob Dillingham (7) gestures after scoring during the first half against the Los Angeles Clippers at Intuit Dome." style="display:block; max-width:100%; height:auto; margin:0 0 1em 0;" decoding="async" loading="lazy" />Just two years after becoming a lottery pick, Rob Dillingham is without an NBA contract following three stops.]]></description>
										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<img width="1024" height="683" src="https://fadeawayworld.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Rob-Dillingham.--1024x683.jpg" class="attachment-large size-large wp-post-image" alt="Chicago Bulls guard Rob Dillingham (7) gestures after scoring during the first half against the Los Angeles Clippers at Intuit Dome." style="display:block; max-width:100%; height:auto; margin:0 0 1em 0;" decoding="async" loading="lazy" /><p>Rob Dillingham was considered one of the most exciting prospects in the 2024 NBA Draft, with the <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/nba/minnesota-timberwolves" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Minnesota Timberwolves</a> sacrificing future draft capital to acquire him with the eighth overall pick. But just two years later, the 21-year-old finds himself without an NBA contract after three franchises moved on from him.</p>
<p>NBA draft analyst and scout Rafael Barlowe <a href="https://www.nbabigboard.com/p/did-the-nba-give-up-on-rob-dillingham" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">recently examined Dillingham&#8217;s remarkable downfall</a>, and he questioned whether NBA teams have become impatient with young prospects or whether the original evaluations from scouts were really flawed.</p>
<p><em>&#8220;During that draft process, I was completely sold on Rob Dillingham as a lottery pick. And when Minnesota made the move to acquire him on draft night, I initially thought he had landed in a good situation.&#8221;</em></p>
<p><em>&#8220;He would have an opportunity to learn behind Mike Conley, a smaller point guard who had built a long and successful NBA career. I thought Conley would be the perfect veteran mentor for Dillingham, someone who could help him understand how to run an NBA offense, manage games and make the adjustments necessary to succeed at the next level.&#8221;</em></p>
<p><em>&#8220;But looking back, I wonder if I underestimated just how difficult that situation would be.&#8221;</em></p>
<p>The Wolves acquired Dillingham&#8217;s draft rights from the <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/nba/san-antonio-spurs" target="_blank" rel="noopener">San Antonio Spurs</a> in exchange for a protected 2030 first-round pick swap and an unprotected 2031 first-round pick. But Dillingham failed to establish himself in Minnesota&#8217;s rotation before being traded to the <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/nba/chicago-bulls" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Chicago Bulls</a> in February 2026.</p>
<p>The Bulls eventually moved him to the <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/nba/charlotte-hornets" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Charlotte Hornets</a>, who <a href="https://fadeawayworld.net/nba/charlotte-hornets/rob-dillingham-gets-brutal-rejection-from-tatiana-chanell-after-hornets-exit" target="_blank" rel="noopener">waived him in September</a> without giving him a chance to play. His struggles are particularly surprising considering how he played in college.</p>
<p>At Kentucky, Dillingham averaged 15.2 points, 3.9 assists, and 2.9 rebounds while shooting 47.5% from the field and 44.4% from three-point range. His creativity, ball-handling, and scoring instincts had convinced Barlowe and scouts like him that he possessed lottery-level talent.</p>
<p>But Barlowe acknowledged that <a href="https://www.nbabigboard.com/p/rob-dillingham-the-disconnect-between" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">he underestimated how difficult the transition to the NBA</a> would be for Rob Dillingham.</p>
<p><em>&#8220;I knew Dillingham was undersized. I knew there would be defensive challenges. But I was so impressed with his offensive talent that I may have underestimated just how exceptional he would need to be offensively to overcome those limitations.&#8221;</em></p>
<p><em>&#8220;Because there’s a difference between being a gifted offensive player and being so dynamic that an NBA team is willing to live with everything you give up on the defensive end.&#8221;</em></p>
<p>Standing approximately 6-foot-2 and weighing 170 pounds, Dillingham struggles against bigger and stronger NBA guards. His offensive production has not compensated for his defensive disadvantages.</p>
<p>Across his first 114 NBA appearances, Dillingham shot approximately 32.4% from three-point range. He averaged 9.6 points and 2.8 assists in 30 games with the Chicago Bulls, shooting around 30.0% from beyond the arc.</p>
<p>Barlowe also recalled a veteran NBA scout repeatedly warning him about Dillingham&#8217;s limitations before the draft.</p>
<p><em>&#8220;Throughout the 2024 draft process, almost every time I wrote an article praising Dillingham, a friend of mine who is a veteran NBA scout would push back. And his message was always the same.&#8221;</em></p>
<p><em>&#8220;&#8216;Unless he’s Trae Young, he’s not going to make it.'&#8221;</em></p>
<p><em>&#8220;He would explain that even Trae, despite being one of the most dynamic offensive guards in basketball, has struggled defensively because of his size. But Trae is such an exceptional offensive player that teams are willing to live with those limitations. His point was simple: Rob Dillingham wasn’t Trae Young.</em></p>
<p><em>&#8220;And unless Dillingham could become that type of offensive force, he didn’t think Rob would survive in the NBA.&#8221;</em></p>
<p>The scout&#8217;s argument was that undersized guards must have extraordinary offensive talent to justify their defensive issues.</p>
<p>But Barlowe&#8217;s subsequent conversations with NBA player development coaches revealed another possible explanation.</p>
<p>One coach did suggest that Dillingham&#8217;s unconventional scoring style required an environment that allowed him to play more freely.</p>
<p><em>&#8220;Rob needs freedom. He needs a system that allows him to be creative and maybe carve out his own role as a misfit small scoring guard who maybe doesn’t play every night but can change a game in January with his elite speed and burst of scoring.&#8221;</em></p>
<p><em>&#8220;And because a role like that currently doesn’t exist on most teams, he may never fit.&#8221;</em></p>
<p>With Minnesota competing for a title, those opportunities were difficult to come by, as head coach Chris Finch prioritized immediate victories over development chances.</p>
<p>Another development coach questioned whether organizations are sufficiently prepared to develop players whose strengths don&#8217;t fit traditional NBA roles.</p>
<p><em>&#8220;Does Rob have to adjust and conform to what the NBA wants him to be, a run-your-team, low-turnover, high-assist, high-three-point-percentage efficient guard who picks up 94 feet to combat his lack of size? Absolutely.&#8221;</em></p>
<p><em>&#8220;But if you scouted Rob and watched him since high school and college, and you know those aren’t his strengths, instead of boxing him into someone he isn’t, why not create an environment for him that will help him succeed?&#8221;</em></p>
<p><em>&#8220;Allow him to create his own lane and provide a structure where, yes, he can grow into what makes sense for the organization, but also allow him to be himself to an extent that doesn’t require overhauling what you do team wise.&#8221;</em></p>
<p><em>&#8220;I feel like that’s the responsibility of the organization when you draft a kid top 10 or so.”</em></p>
<p>Barlowe thinks both the player and his former organization deserve some blame. Dillingham must improve his shooting, decision-making, and defensive effectiveness, but the Timberwolves also invested a substantial amount of draft capital in a prospect who clearly needed time.</p>
<p>The analyst additionally acknowledged that Kentucky head coach John Calipari might have maximized Dillingham&#8217;s strengths by using him as a sixth man without necessarily preparing him to become a traditional NBA point guard.</p>
<p>That distinction could explain why his impressive college production hasn&#8217;t translated into professional success. Nevertheless, Barlowe remains hopeful that Dillingham can revive his career.</p>
<p>At 21, he still has a lot of the creativity and scoring ability that made him a lottery prospect, but his next opportunity will depend on finding an organization willing to develop those strengths while helping him address his weaknesses.</p>
<p>If he does not get a chance in the NBA in the near future, he might have to look overseas, maybe to Europe or China, to develop and improve his stock before he gets another NBA chance.</p>
<p>But for now, his journey serves as a reminder that identifying NBA talent and successfully developing it are two very different and difficult challenges.</p>
